# Interzone Content Voice — Full Rules

## The World We're Describing
Camp Interzone evokes the International Zone of 1950s Tangier, Morocco — a diplomatically neutral port city where expatriate writers (Burroughs, Ginsberg, Bowles), artists, and wanderers gathered to reinvent themselves outside the reach of any single government or moral code. It was a place of radical tolerance, creative ferment, and freedom from judgment.

On the playa, Interzone is a hidden café: warm lantern light, cold beer, a library of free books, live music, morning yoga, evening philosophy. It exists outside the normal rules of the world, as Tangier once did.

## Voice Characteristics
- **Literary, not academic** — evocative sentences, not explanatory ones
- **Cultured, not pretentious** — the voice of a well-traveled host, not a professor
- **Mysterious, not obscure** — intrigue that invites, never excludes
- **Warm, not cloying** — welcoming without being effusive
- **Confident, not aggressive** — the camp knows what it is; it doesn't shout

## Tense & Person
- Present tense throughout
- Second person ("you find yourself…", "you are welcome here")
- Active voice

## Sentence Style
- Short to medium sentences
- Declarative constructions: "The library is free. The beer is cold. You are welcome."
- Allow occasional sentence fragments for effect
- No exclamation points. No emoji.

## Vocabulary
### Reach for:
- Tangier, the International Zone, the medina
- Wanderers, travelers, expatriates
- The playa, the desert, the dust
- Café, lantern, threshold, passage
- Free, gifted, offered without expectation
- Reinvention, freedom, tolerance, the open road
- Quiet, still, cool shade in the heat

### Avoid:
- "Community" (overused in Burning Man context)
- "Vibes", "energy", "space" (as in "holding space")
- "Journey" used metaphorically
- Marketing speak: "world-class", "curated", "unique experience"
- Excessive Burning Man jargon (gifting economy, radical self-expression) — evoke the spirit without the clichés
- Burroughs references that require explanation — the allusion should land, not need a footnote

## Section-Specific Notes

### Hero
Short, cinematic. 5–10 words max for the headline. The tagline is a whisper, not a shout.
Example direction: "A hidden café at the edge of the known world."

### About / The Story
2–3 paragraphs. Opens with Tangier, pivots to the playa, arrives at an invitation.
Never explain what Burning Man is — assume the reader knows.

### The Lost Times Library
Lead with the books, then the act of giving. The library is named after a fictional Tangier newspaper; lean into that.
The gift is unconditional — no strings, no sign-up, no transaction.

### Library Loud Hours
Music + beer + the afternoon heat. Write it like you're already there.
Cold draft beer is a gift. Live music is a gift. The afternoon is the gift.

### Workshops & Events
Matter-of-fact listing with poetic framing. Buddhism sits next to board games without irony.
These are invitations, not schedules.

### Gifting Culture
The philosophical anchor of the site. Short. Precise. The economy of gift — nothing expected in return.

### Join Form
The form is styled as a telegram or library card. The intro copy should feel like receiving an invitation in the mail.
Formal, warm, brief.

## Length Guidelines
- Hero headline: 5–10 words
- Section headlines (H2): 3–6 words
- Section body copy: 80–150 words per section
- Form intro: 30–50 words
- Footer: 1 line + navigation
